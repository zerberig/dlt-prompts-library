Context: I'm defining [product name], an online tool that solves [problem it solves] for [target users]. The first version needs to launch within [launch timeline] with a team of [team size].
Goal: A product requirements document (PRD) that aligns design, engineering, and business on what to build first and how we'll know it worked.
Deliverable: A PRD with these sections: 1) Summary and value proposition. 2) Problem and evidence (what we know and what still needs validating). 3) Users and key scenarios. 4) Goals and success metrics. 5) Prioritized functional requirements (must-have, nice-to-have, future) with user stories and acceptance criteria. 6) Non-functional requirements (performance, security, privacy, accessibility). 7) Out of scope. 8) Risks, dependencies, and open questions. 9) Milestones through launch.
Criteria: Concrete and testable: every requirement must be verifiable. Clearly separate facts from assumptions. If the product handles personal data, flag the privacy obligations worth reviewing with a legal specialist.
Format: Headings per section; functional requirements as a table (ID, User story, Priority, Acceptance criteria). 1,500 to 2,500 words.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
