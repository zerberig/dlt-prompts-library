Context: I run a YouTube channel about [channel topic] for [target audience]. The next video is about [video topic] and will run [video length]. My on-camera style is [presenting style].
Goal: I want a script that keeps people watching to the end, because retention is what tells the platform the video is worth recommending, and that leaves them wanting more of the channel.
Deliverable:
1) 3 alternative hooks for the first 15 seconds that state the video's promise without beating around the bush.
2) The full script, split into sections with subheadings, written to be spoken aloud.
3) At least 3 curiosity loops (a question raised and answered later) and a change of pace every 60-90 seconds (example, story, demo, graphic, joke).
4) Visual support cues in parentheses (on-screen asset, shot change, text).
5) An ending that delivers the promise and points to a related video instead of a long goodbye.
Criteria: Deliver everything the hook promises, because overpromising and underdelivering sinks retention. Get straight into the content; no long intros and no asking for subscriptions at the start. If you include data, flag as an estimate any figure you can't back up with a source.
Format: Hooks as a list, then the script by section with approximate timestamps.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
