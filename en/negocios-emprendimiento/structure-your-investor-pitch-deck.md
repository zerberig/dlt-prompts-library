Context: I'm raising [amount to raise] for [your company or project], which solves [problem you solve] for its customers. My current traction is [traction and key data], and I'll be pitching to [type of investor].
Goal: I want a clear, compelling deck that lets an investor grasp the opportunity in a few minutes and want a second meeting.
Deliverable: A 10-to-12-slide pitch deck: problem, solution, why now, market size, product, business model, traction, competition and differentiation, growth strategy, team, financial projections, and use of funds. For each slide: a headline that states a message (not a label), 3 to 5 content points, a suggested chart or visual, and speaker notes to present it in under a minute. At the end, the 5 toughest questions I'm likely to get and how to answer them.
Criteria: Use only the data I gave you; if something is missing, leave a placeholder marked "to be completed". Projections should be defensible with visible assumptions; flag as an estimate any figure you can't back up with a source. Recommend that I review the deal terms with a lawyer or advisor.
Format: One section per slide with Headline, Content, Visual, and Notes; the tough questions as a numbered list.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
