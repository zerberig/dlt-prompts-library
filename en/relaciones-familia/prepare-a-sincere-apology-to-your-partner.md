Context: My partner and I had a misunderstanding. Here's what happened: [what happened]. I think they felt [how you think they felt]. We usually communicate best [how you communicate best] (in person, in writing, after cooling off for a while).
Goal: Make an apology that actually repairs things, not one that just closes the topic.
Deliverable: 1) A short guide to the elements of an effective apology applied to my case: acknowledging what I did, its impact, taking responsibility without justifying myself, and what I'll do differently. 2) A draft of what I can say, in my own words, 100-150 words long. 3) Phrases to avoid ("sorry if you were offended", "yes, but you...") and why. 4) How to listen to their response and what to do if they're not ready to talk yet.
Criteria: Honest, human tone, nothing theatrical. If what I describe involves violence, control, or a conflict that keeps repeating, suggest I seek support from a couples therapist.
Format: Numbered sections; the draft in a separate block.
