Context: I'm hiring for a [role and level] position at [type of company or team]. The day-to-day work involves [main technologies and responsibilities].
Goal: A structured, fair interview that lets me compare candidates on the same criteria and predict real on-the-job performance.
Deliverable: 1) 6 technical questions on fundamentals, the stack, and debugging, tailored to the level. 2) 4 behavioral questions on teamwork, problem-solving, and learning. 3) 3 questions about past projects. 4) For each question: what it assesses, signs of a strong answer, and red flags. 5) A 1-to-4 scoring rubric and a 60-minute agenda.
Criteria: Questions grounded in real situations from the role rather than puzzles or trivia, because they predict performance better. For junior levels, weigh reasoning and eagerness to learn above experience. Leave out questions about age, marital status, origin, or other personal topics that could lead to discrimination.
Format: One table per section with the columns Question, What it assesses, Strong answer, and Red flag; the rubric and agenda at the end.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
