Context: I work at [your organization], in the [industry] sector, and we manage [platforms]. The team available for a crisis is [available team], and we don't have a formal plan today.
Goal: I want a crisis response plan the team can follow without improvising, so we react quickly, with empathy, and without mistakes that make things worse.
Deliverable: 1) An incident classification with 3 severity levels, examples from my industry, and the maximum first-response time for each. 2) Crisis committee roles and responsibilities (who monitors, drafts, approves, and acts as spokesperson), with backups. 3) A decision flow from detection to close. 4) The most common mistakes to avoid. 5) Base templates for the first response, updates, and the closing message. 6) How to run the post-crisis review.
Criteria: Keep it practical and short enough to fit in a few pages; favor checklists over theory. Make empathy an explicit criterion in every response, and define when to pause scheduled posts. Note which cases require legal counsel before posting.
Format: A document with numbered sections; severity levels and roles in tables; the decision flow as a numbered list.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
