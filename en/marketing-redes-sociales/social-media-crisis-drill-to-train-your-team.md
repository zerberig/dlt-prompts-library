Context: I lead the team that runs social media for [your organization] on [platforms]. The team includes [team roles], and our most likely risks are [main risks] (a service outage, a viral complaint, an employee's poorly judged comment, etc.).
Goal: I want a hands-on training module of about two hours where the team practices responding to a crisis before facing a real one.
Deliverable: 1) Learning objectives for the module. 2) Three drill scenarios of increasing difficulty, each with the starting situation, two or three twists revealed during the exercise, and the decisions the team must make. 3) Role assignments during the drill: who monitors, who drafts, who approves, and who escalates. 4) An evaluation rubric and a guide for the debrief session.
Criteria: Scenarios should be realistic for my type of organization and fictional in their details, without naming real people. Include time pressure and conflicting information, because that's how real crises feel. For scenarios with legal implications, note when to bring in legal counsel.
Format: An agenda with times, each scenario in its own section, and the rubric in a table with the columns Criterion, Strong, Adequate, and Needs work.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
