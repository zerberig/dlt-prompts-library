Context: I'm going to run a campaign for [your brand or business] on [platforms], targeting [country or countries]. I plan to use these materials I didn't create: [list of third-party materials] (for example, a trending song, photos from the internet, a user's video, a font, a meme, another brand's logo), and this is what I know about their origin and license: [what you know about each material].
Goal: I want to know what I can use as is, what needs a license or permission, and what I should replace, before I publish.
Deliverable: 1) A plain-language explanation of the key concepts: copyright, licenses, public domain, Creative Commons, usage exceptions (such as fair use or the right of quotation, which vary by country), and the difference between personal and commercial use. 2) An assessment of each material with a risk level and what to do. 3) Safe alternatives for high-risk materials. 4) How to document permissions and licenses.
Criteria: Keep in mind that marketing use counts as commercial and has stricter rules, and that each platform's music library may have restrictions for business accounts. If you have web search, check my country's laws and the platforms' current rules; flag anything you can't confirm as an estimate. Make clear this is general information and recommend consulting an intellectual property lawyer for high-reach campaigns.
Format: The assessment in a table with the columns Material, Risk (low, medium, or high), Why, and What to do; the rest in short sections.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
