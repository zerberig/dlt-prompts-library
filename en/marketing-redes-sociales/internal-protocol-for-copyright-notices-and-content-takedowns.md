Context: I lead a team that manages [number of accounts or brands] on [platforms]. Right now, when a copyright infringement notice comes in, everyone reacts however they can and we don't keep records.
Goal: I want a standard procedure anyone on the team can follow to respond properly to a notice, protect account health, and lower the chance it happens again.
Deliverable: 1) The steps from the moment a notice arrives until the case is closed, with an owner and deadline for each. 2) A decision tree for choosing between removing the content, getting a license, or filing a counter-notice. 3) What to record in a case log and in a license register. 4) How to track accumulated strikes per account. 5) Prevention measures for the production workflow.
Criteria: If you have web search, check the current notice, strike, and counter-notice process for [platforms]; flag anything you can't confirm as an estimate. Explain that a counter-notice only makes sense when you actually hold rights to the content, because filing one without grounds can have legal consequences. Make clear this is general information and recommend consulting an intellectual property lawyer when there are money claims or threats of legal action.
Format: A document with numbered sections; the steps in a table with the columns Step, Owner, Deadline, and Record; the decision tree as a numbered list.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
