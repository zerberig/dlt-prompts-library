Context: I send [type of emails] to a list of about [list size] contacts. My recent results are: [current metrics] (open rate, clicks, conversion, unsubscribes).
Goal: Stop testing at random and run an organized program that improves results right where I lose the most.
Deliverable: 1) A short diagnosis: which stage (open, click, or conversion) has the most room to improve and why. If you have web search, compare my numbers with recent benchmarks for my industry and cite the source. 2) 10 test ideas prioritized with an ICE score (impact, confidence, ease). 3) A 12-week calendar running one test at a time. 4) A short template for documenting each result.
Criteria: Each test changes one variable and has a written hypothesis ("If I change this, I expect this result, because…"). Keep in mind open rates may be inflated by privacy features. Flag as an estimate any figure you can't back up with a source.
Format: A test table with the columns Test, Hypothesis, Metric, Impact, Confidence, Ease, and Score; the calendar as a week-by-week list.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
