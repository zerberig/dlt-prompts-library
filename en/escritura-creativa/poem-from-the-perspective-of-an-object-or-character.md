Write a first-person poem from the perspective of [who is speaking] (an object, an animal, a place, or a historical figure) as they go through [situation or turning point].
Give it its own coherent voice: what that perspective can see, know, and feel, and what it can't. Reveal its story and emotions through what it notices and the details it chooses to mention, not through explanation. Include at least one moment where the reader grasps something about the speaker that the speaker doesn't quite understand. If it's a historical figure, respect the known facts and save the invention for their inner world.
Deliver a 20 to 30-line poem with a title and a final line identifying the speaker, if the poem didn't make it clear.
