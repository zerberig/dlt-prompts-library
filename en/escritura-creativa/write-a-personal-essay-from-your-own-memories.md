Context: I want to write a personal essay about [experience or topic]. I'll publish it in [where you'll publish it] and it should run about [length in words] words.
Goal: Tell something I lived through in a way other people can see themselves in, without sliding into a sermon or self-help.
Deliverable:
1. First, ask me 6 to 8 reflective questions, one at a time, that lead me to concrete memories: a place, a conversation, an object, the moment something shifted.
2. Using my answers, suggest 2 possible approaches for the essay: which scene opens it, what tension carries it, and what idea it lands on.
3. Once I pick one, write the full draft.
Criteria: Use my words and details; don't invent facts about my life. Favor scenes and sensory detail over abstract reflection, and let the conclusion grow out of the story instead of ending on a moral.
Format: Numbered questions; approaches as two short paragraphs; draft in prose with a title.
