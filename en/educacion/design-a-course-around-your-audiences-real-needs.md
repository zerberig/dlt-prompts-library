Context: I want to create a course on [course topic] for [target audience]. Here's what I know about them: [what you know about your learners] (current level, problems they want to solve, time available, why they'd look for a course).
Goal: Design a course that answers real needs rather than what I think they should learn, so learners finish it and apply what they learn.
Deliverable: 1) A needs analysis: the gaps between what they know today and what they need to be able to do, plus 5 questions to validate those gaps with potential learners before producing anything. 2) 4 to 6 learning outcomes written as observable actions. 3) A course structure derived from those outcomes: modules and the main hands-on activity for each. 4) How to check progress in each module with a concrete piece of evidence.
Criteria: Every module connects explicitly to a need from the analysis; if something doesn't serve any need, suggest cutting it. Favor practice and application over theory. Flag the assumptions I should confirm with my audience.
Format: Four numbered sections; the structure as a table with the columns Module, Need addressed, Learning outcome, and Evidence of progress. 500–800 words.
If any essential information is missing, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
